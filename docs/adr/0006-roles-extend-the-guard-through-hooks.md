# Roles extend the guard through hooks

A freeze authority that freezes one account at a time keeps each frozen account at an actor of its own, a marker, since a list in the config would make every message dearer with each account frozen and a cap on it would run out against fresh accounts. The guard must then send a check to the caller's marker, have the marker answer it, keep program messages off the markers, and keep any handler from writing a mark. All of that is routing, and the routing is the guard's. So the guard gains hooks, points where a role adds to what the guard does beside its policy, and the freeze builds its markers on them. A role's hooks are a trait of their own, `Hooks`. The engine keeps its own rules around each hook, so a role cannot break another role's guarantees. The guard has four. In the first two, a role's consent in `Hooks::may_run` and its own operation in `Hooks::operate` return sync messages along with their answer. The config sends the consent's messages ahead of the handler's own, or alone at a request for clearance, and an operation's with the config it writes. `Ctx::config` gives a role the config's actor. The third is a role's own message, `Ctx::own_message`, which the config sends to one of the role's own actors, and which that actor hands to the role's `Hooks::receive` with its own state, never to the handler. A role's own actor for a 32-byte key is the PDA of the program under a seed that hashes a fixed tag, the config's account, the key and the role's name. The engine derives it, and the receiving guard refuses an own message anywhere else (E141), so a role writes only its own actors, never a seat, a balance or another role's actor. A freeze's marker is its own actor for the frozen account's id, so the seed the freeze would have chosen for itself is the engine's, and the freeze chooses the key. The own actor takes the message only from the config (E123), and a handler that sends one in the program is refused (E140), so only the role's consent or operation reaches the role's rules there. A role whose messages include any other to an actor of its own program, or its own to the config, is refused (E139). Every actor of the program takes any other message from the config as already gated, a `#[from_config]` message and one that needs a holder alike, and the config takes a hop from any of them, so a role sending there could act past the gates of the other roles. The fourth is a pair of looks around every handler, at every actor of the program and for exempt messages too. `Hooks::may_handle` sees the actor's state just before the handler, and `Hooks::may_write` sees it after, with the state the handler wrote. So a freeze keeps the program off its marks, and its refusal comes before a handler that cannot read a mark fails on it. The look after the handler keeps any handler from writing a mark, which would freeze an account nobody froze. Neither look is told which actor it runs at, so a role refuses by the state it recognizes. Both sit behind one switch, `Hooks::WATCHES_STATE`, so a program whose roles leave it unset pays nothing.

## Considered options

- **Markers in the engine,** behind one switch on the role that refuses program messages. The engine would own the tag, the seed, the check, mark and unmark messages and the write rule, and the role would supply its codes. Less public surface, but the engine would decide what a role may do with its markers, and the next role with a need of its own would wait for the engine again.
- **One interceptor around every step,** with the role seeing the whole input and the handler's response and free to refuse, rewrite or add messages. The most open, and a role could undo the engine's rules for the other roles, such as the slot check or the ban on library messages to the config.
- **An own message at any actor but the config,** with the role choosing the receiver. No hash at the receiver, and the role's seed its own, but every role crate would be trusted with every seat and balance of the program, as its handler is, and a role could overwrite another role's guarantees.
- **One look, after the handler only.** One method fewer, since `Hooks::may_write` already sees the state before the handler. But a handler that cannot read a mark, such as a credit that decodes the balance it adds to, panics before the look, and the client sees the handler's panic in place of the role's refusal.
- **A list in the config,** read by `may_run` with no new hook. Free while nothing is frozen, and every message pays for each account frozen, about 190 cycles per read of the config with the records stored raw.
