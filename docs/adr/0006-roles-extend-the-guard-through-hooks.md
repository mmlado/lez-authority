# Roles extend the guard through hooks

A freeze authority that freezes one account at a time keeps each frozen account at an actor of its own, a marker, since a list in the config would make every message dearer with each account frozen and a cap on it would run out against fresh accounts. The guard must then send a check to the caller's marker, have the marker answer it, and keep program messages off the markers. All of that is routing, and the routing is the guard's. So the guard gains hooks, points where a role's policy adds to what the guard does, and the freeze builds its markers on them. The engine keeps its own rules around each hook, so a role cannot break another role's guarantees. Four hooks are planned. The first two land with this record: a role's consent in `Policy::may_run` and its own operation in `Policy::operate` return sync messages along with their answer. The config sends the consent's messages ahead of the handler's own, or alone at a request for clearance, and an operation's with the config it writes. A role whose messages include one to any actor of its own program is refused (E139). Every actor of the program takes a message from the config as already gated, a `#[from_config]` message and one that needs a holder alike, and the config takes a hop from any of them, so a role sending there could act past the gates of the other roles. The role's rules pass no other role's gate, so they reach only other programs. `Ctx::config` gives a role the config's actor. The other two hooks come next: a role's own message handled at an actor other than the config, accepted only from the config, which is the one way a role reaches its program's actors, and a look at the state before and after every program handler, behind a switch on `Policy` so a program that does not use it pays nothing.

## Considered options

- **Markers in the engine,** behind one switch on the role that refuses program messages. The engine would own the tag, the seed, the check, mark and unmark messages and the write rule, and the role would supply its codes. Less public surface, but the engine would decide what a role may do with its markers, and the next role with a need of its own would wait for the engine again.
- **One interceptor around every step,** with the role seeing the whole input and the handler's response and free to refuse, rewrite or add messages. The most open, and a role could undo the engine's rules for the other roles, such as the slot check or the ban on library messages to the config.
- **A list in the config,** read by `may_run` with no new hook. Free while nothing is frozen, and every message pays for each account frozen, about 190 cycles per read of the config with the records stored raw.
